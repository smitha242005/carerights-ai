About

How CareRights AI works

The problem it solves, and how it stays honest.

The problem

A lot of health decisions come with a hidden legal or financial question attached — an insurance denial, a delayed treatment, a doctor saying a procedure is needed while the insurer says it isn't. Today, resolving this means separately consulting a doctor and a lawyer or insurance expert — slow, expensive, and often abandoned. Insurers already have automatic claim-processing systems, but those work for the insurer's speed, not the patient's fairness. CareRights AI works on the patient's side of that gap — before filing, to strengthen a case, and after a denial, to fight back with real rights information.

How it stays grounded, not guessing

Every agent retrieves real source documents before answering — real medical guidelines, real insurance regulations — through a technique called retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). If the available sources don't clearly cover your situation, the agent says so honestly instead of inventing an answer. You can see exactly which source chunk supported each finding.

Why three agents, not one

Medical necessity, insurance entitlement, and urgency are three different kinds of questions. Keeping them as separate agents — instead of blending them into one answer — means you see exactly where they agree and where they clash, which is often the most useful information in a real dispute.

What this is not

CareRights AI does not diagnose medical conditions and does not give a legal ruling. It organizes real evidence and shows you what to ask your doctor or insurer next. For anything urgent or high-risk, it explicitly stops and tells you to seek real help immediately.
