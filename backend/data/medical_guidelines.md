# Medical Necessity Guidelines — Starter Corpus (50 sections)
# NOTE: These are original plain-language summaries of well-established, publicly known
# clinical practice patterns, written for this prototype. They are NOT a substitute for
# licensed clinical guidelines (e.g. NICE, WHO, ICMR) and MUST be replaced with properly
# licensed/sourced guideline text before any real-world clinical use.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-001
TOPIC: Knee osteoarthritis and joint replacement surgery
CONTENT: For knee osteoarthritis, conservative management is typically tried first: weight
management, physical therapy/physiotherapy, anti-inflammatory medication, and activity
modification, usually for a period of at least 3 to 6 months. Joint replacement (arthroplasty)
surgery is generally considered medically appropriate when conservative treatment has failed
to control pain or restore function, and imaging confirms significant joint damage. Failed
physiotherapy of 6 months or more is commonly cited as sufficient justification for surgical
referral.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-002
TOPIC: MRI imaging for headache
CONTENT: Most headaches, including migraines and tension headaches, do not require MRI
imaging and are diagnosed clinically. MRI for headache is generally considered medically
necessary when there are "red flag" features: sudden severe onset ("thunderclap" headache),
new headache after age 50, headache with fever/neurological symptoms/vision changes,
headache worsening despite treatment, or history of cancer/immunosuppression. Routine
imaging for typical migraine without red flags is usually not considered necessary by
standard clinical criteria.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-003
TOPIC: Chest pain and cardiac emergency triage
CONTENT: Chest pain accompanied by shortness of breath, sweating, pain radiating to the
arm/jaw, or a feeling of pressure/tightness is treated as a potential medical emergency
requiring immediate evaluation, since it may indicate a heart attack (myocardial infarction).
Standard triage protocols classify this as needing assessment within minutes, not as a
routine or elective matter. Any prior-authorization or approval process should not delay
emergency evaluation of chest pain with these features.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-004
TOPIC: General principle of medical necessity
CONTENT: "Medically necessary" is generally defined across clinical and insurance contexts
as a health care service or supply that is appropriate and needed for the diagnosis or
treatment of a medical condition, consistent with generally accepted standards of medical
practice, not primarily for the convenience of the patient or provider, and the most
appropriate level of service that can safely be provided.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-005
TOPIC: Delay risk in orthopedic conditions
CONTENT: Delaying treatment for progressive joint conditions (such as advanced osteoarthritis
requiring replacement) can lead to further cartilage loss, muscle deconditioning, and reduced
surgical outcomes the longer surgery is postponed, though it is rarely immediately
life-threatening. Delay is a functional/quality-of-life risk rather than an emergency risk
in most such cases.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-006
TOPIC: Delay risk in cardiac symptoms
CONTENT: Delaying evaluation of possible cardiac chest pain carries a high risk of serious
harm or death if the underlying cause is a heart attack, since treatment effectiveness for
myocardial infarction is highly time-sensitive ("time is muscle"). This is considered a
high-urgency, potentially life-threatening delay risk.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-007
TOPIC: Physiotherapy as first-line conservative treatment
CONTENT: Physiotherapy is widely used as a first-line, non-invasive treatment for
musculoskeletal conditions including back pain, joint pain, and post-injury rehabilitation.
Clinical practice generally expects a documented trial of physiotherapy before more invasive
interventions (injections, surgery) are considered appropriate, except in cases of severe
structural damage or emergency presentation.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-008
TOPIC: Hip replacement surgery criteria
CONTENT: Hip replacement is generally considered medically necessary when hip osteoarthritis
or another degenerative condition causes persistent pain and functional limitation (difficulty
walking, climbing stairs) despite at least 3 to 6 months of conservative management including
physiotherapy, weight management, and pain medication, combined with imaging evidence of
significant joint degeneration.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-009
TOPIC: Shoulder rotator cuff surgery
CONTENT: Rotator cuff repair surgery is typically considered appropriate after a documented
trial of conservative treatment (physiotherapy, activity modification, anti-inflammatory
medication) lasting 3 to 6 months has failed, particularly for partial tears. Full-thickness
tears with significant functional loss, or tears following acute trauma, may justify earlier
surgical referral without a full conservative trial.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-010
TOPIC: Spinal disc herniation and back surgery
CONTENT: Most cases of lumbar disc herniation improve with conservative management
(physiotherapy, pain control, activity modification) within 6 to 12 weeks. Surgery is
generally considered medically necessary when there is significant nerve compression causing
progressive weakness, loss of bladder/bowel control (a surgical emergency called cauda equina
syndrome), or persistent disabling pain that has not responded to at least 6 weeks of
conservative treatment.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-011
TOPIC: ACL tear and ligament reconstruction
CONTENT: Anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction is generally considered medically
necessary for active individuals with a confirmed complete tear causing instability, especially
if they wish to return to sports or physically demanding work. For low-demand patients or
partial tears, a trial of physiotherapy-based rehabilitation is often appropriate before
considering surgery.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-012
TOPIC: Fracture management
CONTENT: Most simple fractures are managed with immobilization (casting or splinting) and do
not require surgery. Surgical fixation is generally considered medically necessary for
fractures that are displaced, unstable, involve a joint surface, or fail to heal properly with
conservative management. Open fractures (bone breaking through skin) and fractures with nerve
or blood vessel involvement are treated as surgical emergencies.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-013
TOPIC: Hypertension management
CONTENT: Hypertension (high blood pressure) is generally managed first with lifestyle
modification (diet, exercise, salt reduction) for mild cases, with medication introduced when
blood pressure remains elevated despite these measures, or immediately for more severe
readings. Regular monitoring is considered medically necessary given the long-term risk of
heart disease, stroke, and kidney damage if left uncontrolled.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-014
TOPIC: Angioplasty and cardiac stent necessity
CONTENT: Coronary angioplasty with stenting is generally considered medically necessary when
imaging (such as angiography) confirms significant blockage of a coronary artery, particularly
when accompanied by symptoms (chest pain, reduced exercise tolerance) or evidence of reduced
blood flow to heart muscle. In the setting of an acute heart attack, timely angioplasty is
considered a time-critical, emergency intervention.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-015
TOPIC: Heart failure treatment
CONTENT: Heart failure is generally managed with a combination of medication, dietary sodium
restriction, and monitoring of weight and symptoms. Hospitalization is considered medically
necessary for acute decompensation (sudden worsening) marked by severe shortness of breath,
significant fluid retention, or low oxygen levels, as this can be life-threatening if untreated.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-016
TOPIC: Arrhythmia and pacemaker criteria
CONTENT: Pacemaker implantation is generally considered medically necessary for confirmed
symptomatic bradycardia (abnormally slow heart rate) or certain heart block conditions,
particularly when accompanied by fainting, dizziness, or extreme fatigue. Asymptomatic minor
rhythm irregularities typically do not require device implantation and are monitored instead.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-017
TOPIC: Cardiac bypass surgery (CABG) criteria
CONTENT: Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is generally considered medically necessary
when angiography shows severe blockage in multiple coronary arteries or the main left
coronary artery, especially when angioplasty/stenting is not a suitable option or has already
failed, and the patient has significant symptoms or reduced heart function.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-018
TOPIC: Stroke recognition and emergency window
CONTENT: Stroke symptoms are commonly recognized using the "FAST" criteria: Face drooping, Arm
weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency services. Stroke is a medical emergency
because clot-dissolving treatment is only effective within a narrow time window (commonly
cited as within 4.5 hours of symptom onset for certain treatments), making immediate
evaluation critical rather than routine or elective.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-019
TOPIC: Migraine versus tension headache distinction
CONTENT: Migraine is typically characterized by moderate-to-severe throbbing pain, often
one-sided, with nausea and sensitivity to light/sound, while tension headache is typically
milder, band-like pressure without these associated symptoms. Both are usually diagnosed
clinically without imaging unless red-flag features are present (see MED-002).

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-020
TOPIC: Seizure and epilepsy management
CONTENT: A single unprovoked seizure is typically evaluated with neurological assessment and
imaging, but ongoing anti-seizure medication is generally considered medically necessary only
after a diagnosis of epilepsy (recurrent unprovoked seizures) is confirmed, or after a single
seizure with a high risk of recurrence based on clinical findings. A seizure lasting more than
5 minutes, or repeated seizures without recovery in between (status epilepticus), is a medical
emergency.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-021
TOPIC: Peripheral neuropathy workup
CONTENT: Peripheral neuropathy (nerve damage causing numbness, tingling, or weakness,
commonly in the hands or feet) is generally evaluated with blood tests and nerve conduction
studies when symptoms are persistent or progressive, particularly to identify treatable causes
such as diabetes or vitamin deficiency. Routine imaging is not typically the first step.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-022
TOPIC: Asthma exacerbation management
CONTENT: Mild asthma flare-ups are generally managed with inhaled bronchodilator medication at
home. Emergency evaluation is considered medically necessary when there is severe shortness of
breath, inability to speak in full sentences, blue-tinged lips, or no improvement with rescue
inhaler use, as this indicates a potentially life-threatening exacerbation.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-023
TOPIC: Pneumonia treatment criteria
CONTENT: Mild pneumonia in an otherwise healthy person is often treated with oral antibiotics
on an outpatient basis. Hospitalization is generally considered medically necessary for
pneumonia with low oxygen levels, difficulty breathing, confusion, very young or elderly
patients, or underlying health conditions that increase risk of complications.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-024
TOPIC: COPD exacerbation
CONTENT: Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) flare-ups are generally managed first
with increased use of bronchodilators and, if needed, a short course of oral steroids.
Hospitalization is considered medically necessary when there is severe breathlessness, low
oxygen saturation, or signs of respiratory failure that do not respond to initial home
treatment.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-025
TOPIC: Sleep apnea and CPAP necessity
CONTENT: CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure) therapy is generally considered medically
necessary following a sleep study (polysomnography) confirming moderate-to-severe obstructive
sleep apnea, particularly when accompanied by symptoms such as excessive daytime sleepiness or
when other health conditions (e.g. hypertension) are worsened by untreated apnea.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-026
TOPIC: Gallbladder removal (cholecystectomy) criteria
CONTENT: Gallbladder removal is generally considered medically necessary for symptomatic
gallstones causing recurrent pain (biliary colic), or for acute cholecystitis (gallbladder
inflammation/infection), which is treated as an urgent surgical condition. Asymptomatic
gallstones found incidentally typically do not require surgery.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-027
TOPIC: Appendicitis and emergency surgery
CONTENT: Acute appendicitis, marked by worsening abdominal pain (often starting near the navel
and moving to the lower right abdomen), fever, and nausea, is treated as a surgical emergency
requiring prompt evaluation and typically prompt removal of the appendix, since delay
significantly raises the risk of rupture and serious infection.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-028
TOPIC: Endoscopy and colonoscopy indications
CONTENT: Upper endoscopy is generally considered medically necessary for persistent reflux
symptoms unresponsive to medication, difficulty swallowing, unexplained weight loss, or
gastrointestinal bleeding. Colonoscopy is generally recommended for screening starting at a
standard age (commonly cited as 45-50 in general population guidelines) or earlier with
symptoms such as rectal bleeding, unexplained anemia, or a strong family history of colon
cancer.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-029
TOPIC: GERD management before surgical referral
CONTENT: Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is generally managed first with lifestyle
changes and acid-suppressing medication for at least 8 to 12 weeks. Surgical treatment (such
as fundoplication) is generally considered medically necessary only when symptoms persist
despite adequate medical therapy, or when complications such as severe esophagitis are present.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-030
TOPIC: Cancer screening guidelines
CONTENT: Standard cancer screening recommendations commonly include mammography for breast
cancer starting around age 40-50 depending on risk factors, and colonoscopy for colorectal
cancer starting around age 45-50, repeated at defined intervals. Screening outside these
windows, or more frequent screening, is generally considered medically necessary only with
elevated risk factors such as strong family history or genetic predisposition.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-031
TOPIC: Chemotherapy necessity
CONTENT: Chemotherapy is generally considered medically necessary when a cancer diagnosis is
confirmed via biopsy and staging, and an oncologist determines that systemic treatment is
appropriate based on cancer type, stage, and spread. Treatment plans and necessity are
determined on a case-by-case basis by the treating specialist, informed by tumor biology and
guideline-based protocols.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-032
TOPIC: Biopsy for suspicious findings
CONTENT: A biopsy (tissue sampling) is generally considered medically necessary when imaging
or physical examination reveals a mass, lump, or lesion with features suspicious for cancer or
another serious condition, since biopsy is typically the only way to obtain a definitive
diagnosis before planning treatment.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-033
TOPIC: Cesarean section medical necessity criteria
CONTENT: Cesarean section is generally considered medically necessary for specific indications
such as fetal distress, abnormal fetal positioning (breech), placenta previa, failure of labor
to progress, or maternal health conditions that make vaginal delivery unsafe. Elective cesarean
without a medical indication is treated differently from a medically necessary one for
clinical and coverage purposes.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-034
TOPIC: High-risk pregnancy monitoring
CONTENT: Pregnancies are generally classified as high-risk, requiring more frequent monitoring
and specialist involvement, when there are pre-existing conditions (such as diabetes or
hypertension), a history of pregnancy complications, multiple gestation (twins or more), or
maternal age at the higher or lower end of the typical reproductive range.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-035
TOPIC: Fertility treatment necessity
CONTENT: Fertility treatments such as IUI or IVF are generally considered appropriate after a
diagnostic workup confirms infertility, commonly defined as failure to conceive after 12
months of regular unprotected intercourse (or 6 months for women over 35), or when a specific
diagnosed cause of infertility is identified earlier.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-036
TOPIC: Childhood vaccination necessity
CONTENT: Routine childhood vaccination is considered medically necessary preventive care
following standard national immunization schedules, protecting against diseases such as
measles, polio, and whooping cough. Vaccination timing follows age-based schedules established
by public health authorities.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-037
TOPIC: Pediatric fever workup
CONTENT: Fever in infants and young children is evaluated differently depending on age; fever
in an infant under 3 months old is generally treated as requiring urgent medical evaluation
regardless of how the child otherwise appears, due to higher risk of serious underlying
infection at this age, while fever in older children is assessed based on associated symptoms
and how unwell the child appears.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-038
TOPIC: Depression and anxiety treatment necessity
CONTENT: Treatment for depression or anxiety, including therapy and/or medication, is
generally considered medically necessary when symptoms are persistent (commonly a minimum
duration such as 2 weeks for depressive symptoms) and cause significant impairment in daily
functioning, as assessed by a qualified mental health professional using standard diagnostic
criteria.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-039
TOPIC: Psychiatric hospitalization criteria
CONTENT: Inpatient psychiatric hospitalization is generally considered medically necessary
when there is imminent risk of harm to self or others, or when a person is unable to care for
their basic needs due to a severe mental health crisis, and cannot be safely managed in an
outpatient setting.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-040
TOPIC: Diabetes management and insulin necessity
CONTENT: Type 2 diabetes is generally managed initially with lifestyle modification and oral
medication, with insulin considered medically necessary when blood sugar remains poorly
controlled despite these measures, or at diagnosis if blood sugar levels are very high. Type 1
diabetes requires insulin from diagnosis, as the body does not produce it.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-041
TOPIC: Thyroid disorder treatment
CONTENT: Hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid) is generally treated with daily hormone
replacement medication once confirmed by blood tests. Hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid) may
be managed with medication, radioactive iodine treatment, or surgery depending on the
underlying cause and severity, determined by an endocrinologist.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-042
TOPIC: Cataract surgery criteria
CONTENT: Cataract surgery is generally considered medically necessary when clouding of the eye
lens causes visual impairment that interferes with daily activities such as reading, driving,
or work, as assessed by an ophthalmologist. Early-stage cataracts causing minimal visual
impact typically do not require immediate surgery.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-043
TOPIC: Tonsillectomy necessity
CONTENT: Tonsillectomy (tonsil removal) is generally considered medically necessary for
recurrent tonsillitis meeting a defined frequency threshold (commonly cited guidance includes
7 or more episodes in 1 year, or 5 or more per year over 2 consecutive years), or for
tonsils large enough to cause breathing obstruction, particularly during sleep.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-044
TOPIC: Dialysis necessity
CONTENT: Dialysis is generally considered medically necessary when kidney function has
declined to a level classified as kidney failure (commonly assessed via a lab measure called
eGFR falling below a defined threshold), or when there are dangerous complications of kidney
failure such as severe fluid overload or dangerously high potassium levels.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-045
TOPIC: Kidney stone treatment
CONTENT: Small kidney stones are often managed conservatively with hydration and pain control,
allowing natural passage. Procedural intervention (such as lithotripsy or surgical removal) is
generally considered medically necessary for stones too large to pass on their own, stones
causing persistent obstruction, or stones associated with infection or worsening kidney
function.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-046
TOPIC: Skin biopsy for suspicious lesions
CONTENT: A skin biopsy is generally considered medically necessary when a mole or skin lesion
shows features concerning for skin cancer, commonly assessed using criteria such as
asymmetry, irregular border, color variation, diameter over 6mm, or evolution/change over
time (the "ABCDE" pattern), since biopsy is the definitive way to rule out malignancy.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-047
TOPIC: Trauma and accident emergency criteria
CONTENT: Significant trauma from accidents, including suspected fractures, head injury with
loss of consciousness, or injuries with heavy bleeding, is treated as requiring emergency
evaluation, since underlying injuries (internal bleeding, spinal injury, traumatic brain
injury) may not be immediately obvious without prompt assessment and imaging.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-048
TOPIC: Burns treatment urgency
CONTENT: Burn severity is generally classified by depth (first, second, or third degree) and
the percentage of body surface area affected. Burns covering a large body surface area, full
thickness (third-degree) burns, or burns to the face, hands, or airway are treated as
emergencies requiring immediate specialized care due to risk of infection, fluid loss, and
scarring.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-049
TOPIC: Sepsis emergency recognition
CONTENT: Sepsis, a life-threatening response to infection, is commonly recognized through
signs such as high or very low body temperature, rapid heart rate, rapid breathing, confusion,
or low blood pressure in the setting of a known or suspected infection. It is treated as a
medical emergency because mortality risk rises significantly with each hour that treatment
(commonly antibiotics and fluids) is delayed.

---
CHUNK_ID: MED-050
TOPIC: Post-operative rehabilitation necessity
CONTENT: Post-operative physical therapy/rehabilitation is generally considered medically
necessary following major orthopedic surgery (such as joint replacement) or other procedures
affecting mobility, to restore function and prevent complications such as stiffness, blood
clots, or muscle wasting. The typical duration and intensity depend on the specific procedure
and the individual's recovery progress.
