# Insurance & Consumer Rights Guidelines — Starter Corpus (India, IRDAI-based, 50 sections)
# NOTE: These are original plain-language summaries based on publicly reported IRDAI
# Master Circular 2024/2025 provisions and general consumer-rights escalation practice,
# written for this prototype, verified via web search as of the project date. Regulations
# change — this MUST be checked against the current official IRDAI circular before any
# real-world use, and is not a substitute for professional legal/insurance advice.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-001
TOPIC: Cashless claim approval timelines
CONTENT: Under IRDAI's 2024 Master Circular, insurers are required to decide on a cashless
pre-authorization request within 1 hour of receiving it from the network hospital. Discharge
approval (final cashless sign-off at the time of leaving hospital) is required within 3 hours
of the hospital sending the final bill. These are regulatory floors that apply to all
IRDAI-licensed health insurers.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-002
TOPIC: Reimbursement claim settlement timeline and interest
CONTENT: For reimbursement claims (where the patient pays first and claims back), insurers
are required to settle the claim within 30 days of receiving the last necessary document.
If the insurer delays payment beyond this window, the policyholder is entitled to interest
on the claim amount, commonly cited as 2% above the applicable bank rate, without needing
to file a separate complaint for the interest itself.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-003
TOPIC: Grounds insurers commonly cite for denial, and documentation
CONTENT: A large share of claim denials or reductions are linked to documentation issues:
missing discharge summary, unclear itemized bills, absent prescriptions, or incomplete
claim forms, rather than the treatment itself being disputed. IRDAI guidance has cautioned
insurers against mechanically rejecting genuine claims solely for incomplete documentation,
and encourages insurers to seek clarification rather than reject outright where possible.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-004
TOPIC: Escalation ladder for a denied or delayed claim
CONTENT: If a claim is denied or delayed, the standard escalation path is: (1) written
complaint to the insurer's own Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO); (2) if unresolved, escalate
via IRDAI's Integrated Grievance Management System (IGMS), also called the Bima Bharosa
platform; (3) if still unresolved, file a formal complaint with the Insurance Ombudsman for
your region, which can handle claims up to a specified monetary limit at no cost to the
policyholder; (4) as a further option, approach a Consumer Forum or civil court.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-005
TOPIC: Moratorium and claim rejection after long-term coverage
CONTENT: After a policyholder has held continuous health coverage for a specified moratorium
period (reduced from 8 years to 5 years under the 2024 Master Circular), insurers generally
cannot reject a claim except in cases of established fraud or proven non-disclosure of
material facts at the time of purchase. This protects long-term policyholders from late-stage
technical rejections.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-006
TOPIC: Pre-existing disease waiting periods
CONTENT: Health insurance policies apply a waiting period before claims related to a
pre-existing disease (a condition the policyholder had before buying the policy) are payable.
Under the IRDAI (Insurance Products) Regulations, 2024, this waiting period is capped at a
maximum of 36 months (3 years), reduced from the previous cap of up to 48 months. Claims
unrelated to the pre-existing condition are generally not affected by this waiting period.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-007
TOPIC: Mental health coverage parity
CONTENT: IRDAI has mandated that health insurance policies treat mental health conditions
on par with physical illness for coverage purposes (parity mandated since 2018). Denial of a
claim purely on the basis that the condition is a mental health condition, when otherwise
covered, is not compliant with this requirement and is reportable to IRDAI.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-008
TOPIC: What "medically necessary" denial by an insurer means for rights
CONTENT: When an insurer denies a claim as "not medically necessary," the policyholder has
the right to request the specific clinical reasoning in writing, submit supporting
documentation from the treating doctor (including guideline references, prior conservative
treatment history, or urgency indicators), and formally appeal through the Grievance
Redressal Officer before escalating further. A denial reason of "not medically necessary"
alone, without supporting clinical rationale from the insurer, is generally considered
insufficient and can be challenged.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-009
TOPIC: Free look period
CONTENT: Under the 2024 Master Circular, every new health insurance policy comes with a
free look period of 30 days (increased from the earlier 15 days), during which the
policyholder can review the policy terms and cancel it for a refund (minus certain
deductions) if it does not meet their expectations. This 30-day free look period also
applies at the time of policy renewal.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-010
TOPIC: Grace period for premium payment
CONTENT: The grace period for paying a renewal premium is standardized as 15 days where
the premium is paid monthly, and 30 days where the premium is paid quarterly, half-yearly,
or annually. Under the 2024 Master Circular, coverage continues during this grace period,
meaning a claim arising during the grace period cannot automatically be denied just because
the premium had not yet been paid.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-011
TOPIC: Day care treatment definition and coverage
CONTENT: Day care treatment refers to a medical or surgical procedure that, due to
technological advancement, does not require 24-hour hospitalization but is still performed
under general or local anesthesia in a hospital or registered day care center. IRDAI requires
insurers to maintain a list of covered day care procedures (commonly 200 or more), though
this list is generally illustrative rather than exhaustive, and day care treatment is
typically covered on par with regular hospitalization, not excluded merely because it is
short in duration.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-012
TOPIC: Domiciliary hospitalization
CONTENT: Domiciliary hospitalization covers medical treatment taken at home for a condition
that would normally require hospital-level care, when the patient cannot be moved to a
hospital or no hospital bed is available. Policies commonly require the home treatment to
continue for a minimum duration (often cited as at least 72 hours/3 consecutive days) and
typically cap this benefit at a percentage of the total sum insured (often around 10%),
rather than paying it in addition to the full sum insured.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-013
TOPIC: Restoration of sum insured
CONTENT: Many modern health insurance policies include a "restore benefit": if the sum
insured (and any cumulative bonus) is fully exhausted by a claim during the policy year,
100% of the base sum insured is automatically reinstated for further claims in the same
year. This restored amount is typically usable for a different illness in the same year and,
in family floater policies, is often shared across insured family members, subject to the
specific policy's wording.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-014
TOPIC: Cumulative bonus / no-claim bonus
CONTENT: A cumulative bonus (also called no-claim bonus) increases the effective sum insured
for each claim-free policy year, as a reward for not making a claim, without increasing the
premium. This bonus is calculated based on the sum insured of the last completed policy year
and can be reduced or reset if a claim is made, depending on the specific policy's terms.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-015
TOPIC: Portability rights
CONTENT: Policyholders have the right to port (switch) their health insurance policy from
one insurer to another, or between different plans of the same insurer, without losing
credit for the waiting periods already served under the original policy. Portability must
generally be initiated well before the policy renewal date to allow the new insurer time to
process the request.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-016
TOPIC: Co-payment and deductible definitions
CONTENT: A co-payment is a fixed percentage of an admissible claim that the policyholder
must bear out of pocket, regardless of the claim amount; it does not reduce the sum insured
itself. A deductible is a fixed amount that must be paid by the policyholder before the
insurer's payment obligation begins. Both are distinct features that may apply to a policy
depending on its terms, and are separate from any sub-limits on specific treatments.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-017
TOPIC: Third Party Administrator (TPA) role
CONTENT: A Third Party Administrator (TPA) is an entity licensed to process health insurance
claims on behalf of the insurer, including verifying documents, coordinating cashless
approval with hospitals, and facilitating reimbursement settlement. The TPA acts on the
insurer's instructions; ultimate responsibility for claim decisions and regulatory compliance
rests with the insurer, not the TPA.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-018
TOPIC: No age cap for purchasing health insurance
CONTENT: Under the 2024 Master Circular, IRDAI has removed the maximum age limit on entry
for purchasing health insurance, and insurers must offer at least one policy with no upper
age restriction. This is intended to ensure elderly individuals can still access health
coverage rather than being excluded purely due to age.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-019
TOPIC: AYUSH treatment coverage
CONTENT: IRDAI has removed sub-limits that previously restricted coverage for AYUSH
treatments (Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy) when received as
inpatient treatment in a registered AYUSH hospital. This generally means AYUSH hospitalization
is to be covered on par with conventional treatment, up to the sum insured, rather than being
capped at a lower sub-limit.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-020
TOPIC: Modern treatment methods coverage
CONTENT: IRDAI guidance requires health insurance policies to cover a defined list of modern
treatment methods (such as robotic surgery, oral chemotherapy, stem cell therapy, and certain
laser and minimally invasive procedures) rather than excluding them as "experimental" or
"unproven," reflecting how medical technology has advanced beyond older policy wordings.
Coverage for these methods is typically subject to the same medical necessity criteria as
conventional treatment.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-021
TOPIC: Cannot refuse policy for serious pre-existing conditions
CONTENT: IRDAI has prohibited insurers from refusing to sell a health insurance policy solely
because an applicant has a serious pre-existing disease or condition, such as heart disease,
cancer, renal failure, or HIV/AIDS. Insurers may still apply the standard pre-existing disease
waiting period (see INS-006) or adjust premium/terms based on risk, but outright refusal to
cover such applicants is not permitted.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-022
TOPIC: Specialized plans for vulnerable and specific groups
CONTENT: IRDAI has directed insurers to design customized health insurance products tailored
to specific groups, including senior citizens, children, students, maternity needs, and
persons with disabilities, rather than offering only generic one-size-fits-all policies.
Insurers are also expected to set up specialized channels to handle claims and grievances
from senior citizen policyholders.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-023
TOPIC: Cashless treatment at any hospital (Cashless Everywhere)
CONTENT: Under IRDAI's "Cashless Everywhere" initiative (effective from January 2024),
policyholders can avail cashless treatment even at hospitals that are not part of the
insurer's specific network, not only at empanelled network hospitals as was previously the
norm. This significantly expands where a policyholder can receive cashless treatment rather
than having to pay upfront and claim reimbursement later.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-024
TOPIC: GST exemption on individual health insurance premiums
CONTENT: Following a decision of the GST Council, individual and retail health insurance
premiums became exempt from Goods and Services Tax (GST) with effect from September 22,
2025. This is intended to make individual health insurance more affordable by removing the
tax component that was previously added to the premium.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-025
TOPIC: Room rent sub-limits
CONTENT: Some older or budget health insurance policies impose a sub-limit on daily room rent
(for example, capping the eligible room rent as a percentage of the sum insured). When the
policyholder chooses a room with a rent above this sub-limit, other associated charges
(such as doctor fees, nursing charges, and consumables) are often proportionately reduced as
well under a "proportionate deduction" clause, not just the room charge itself. Reviewing
this clause before hospitalization can significantly affect out-of-pocket costs.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-026
TOPIC: Pre- and post-hospitalization expense coverage
CONTENT: Most health insurance policies cover medical expenses incurred in a defined period
before hospitalization (commonly a range such as 30 days) and after discharge (commonly a
range such as 60 to 90 days), covering related diagnostic tests, doctor consultations, and
medication connected to the same illness or injury that led to hospitalization, subject to
the specific policy's stated limits.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-027
TOPIC: Network versus non-network hospital claims process
CONTENT: At a network (empanelled) hospital, a policyholder can generally use the cashless
facility, where the insurer or TPA settles the bill directly with the hospital. At a
non-network hospital, or in cases where cashless is not approved, the policyholder typically
pays first and files a reimbursement claim afterward. The "Cashless Everywhere" provision
(see INS-023) has narrowed, but not entirely eliminated, this distinction.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-028
TOPIC: Family floater versus individual policy
CONTENT: An individual health insurance policy provides a separate sum insured for one
person, while a family floater policy provides a single shared sum insured that can be used
by any covered family member, either for one large claim or split across multiple smaller
claims in the same policy year. Family floater premiums are often lower per person but can be
exhausted faster if multiple family members claim in the same year.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-029
TOPIC: Group health insurance basics
CONTENT: Group health insurance is typically provided by an employer or organization to
cover its members, often with fewer individual underwriting requirements and sometimes with
reduced or waived waiting periods for pre-existing conditions compared to individual retail
policies. Coverage under a group policy generally ends when the individual's association with
the group (such as employment) ends, unless a conversion or portability option to an
individual policy is offered.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-030
TOPIC: Critical illness rider and add-on coverage
CONTENT: A critical illness rider or add-on pays a lump sum benefit upon diagnosis of a
specified serious illness (such as certain cancers, heart attack, or stroke), separate from
and in addition to the standard hospitalization sum insured, provided the specific illness
and its diagnostic criteria are defined in the policy and met.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-031
TOPIC: Maternity coverage waiting period
CONTENT: Maternity benefits in health insurance are commonly subject to their own waiting
period, separate from the general pre-existing disease waiting period, often ranging from
about 9 months to 4 years depending on the specific policy, before delivery-related expenses
become payable. Complications directly arising during pregnancy may sometimes be covered
under general illness provisions even before the maternity-specific waiting period ends,
depending on policy wording.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-032
TOPIC: Organ donor expense coverage
CONTENT: Many health insurance policies cover the medical expenses of an organ donor incurred
for the harvesting/removal of the organ for transplant into the insured person, up to the
insured person's available sum insured, subject to the transplant being conducted in
accordance with applicable organ transplantation laws and the specific policy's terms.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-033
TOPIC: Lifetime renewal guarantee
CONTENT: Every health insurance policy approved under current IRDAI regulations must offer
the policyholder the right to lifetime renewal, meaning the insurer cannot refuse to renew
the policy purely on account of the policyholder's advancing age or claims history, except in
specific circumstances such as established fraud or non-disclosure.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-034
TOPIC: Claim rejection for non-disclosure versus innocent mistake
CONTENT: Insurers may reject a claim, or in serious cases void the policy, if it is
established that the policyholder deliberately concealed or misrepresented a material fact
(such as a known pre-existing condition) at the time of purchase. However, minor or innocent
errors in the proposal form, without intent to deceive, are treated differently and are less
likely to justify outright claim denial, particularly once the moratorium period (see INS-005)
has passed.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-035
TOPIC: Documents required for a reimbursement claim
CONTENT: A typical reimbursement claim requires the completed claim form, original discharge
summary, itemized final hospital bill with payment receipts, original diagnostic/laboratory
reports, prescriptions corresponding to purchased medicines, and the treating doctor's
certificate confirming diagnosis and treatment, though the exact document list can vary
slightly by insurer and case type.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-036
TOPIC: Insurance Ombudsman monetary jurisdiction
CONTENT: The Insurance Ombudsman can adjudicate complaints against insurers, including health
insurance claim disputes, up to a specified monetary limit, and the process is free of cost
to the policyholder. Complaints exceeding this limit, or seeking remedies the Ombudsman
cannot grant, are generally directed to a Consumer Forum or civil court instead.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-037
TOPIC: Consumer Protection Act and deficiency in service
CONTENT: Under the Consumer Protection Act, an insurance policyholder is considered a
"consumer," and unjustified delay or denial of a valid claim can be treated as a "deficiency
in service," giving the policyholder the right to approach a Consumer Forum for redress,
separate from and in addition to the IRDAI/Ombudsman grievance channels.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-038
TOPIC: What happens when sum insured is exhausted mid-year
CONTENT: If a policyholder's sum insured (and any cumulative bonus) is fully used up by
claims during a policy year, further hospitalization expenses in that same year are generally
not payable unless the policy includes a restoration/restore benefit (see INS-013), in which
case the restored amount can be used, typically subject to the specific conditions and
exclusions stated in that policy.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-039
TOPIC: Pre-authorization denial versus final claim denial
CONTENT: A cashless pre-authorization request can be denied by the insurer or TPA without
this being a final rejection of the underlying claim; the policyholder retains the right to
pay for treatment and subsequently file a reimbursement claim, which is assessed on its own
merits and may be approved even if cashless pre-authorization was initially declined.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-040
TOPIC: Bima Sugam and digital insurance infrastructure
CONTENT: IRDAI has been developing a national digital insurance platform, commonly referred
to as Bima Sugam, intended to serve as a common marketplace and repository where
policyholders can purchase, manage, and file claims across insurers, alongside related
digital initiatives such as the Bima-ASBA payment mechanism, aimed at making insurance more
transparent and accessible.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-041
TOPIC: Consumables and non-medical expense exclusions
CONTENT: Certain hospitalization-related items are often classified as "non-medical" or
"consumable" expenses (such as gloves, syringes not tied to a specific treatment, or
administrative charges) and may be excluded or only partially covered under some policies,
depending on IRDAI's list of items that insurers must or must not include as payable, and the
specific policy's wording.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-042
TOPIC: Insurer's right to seek a second medical opinion
CONTENT: For certain claims, particularly high-value or complex ones, an insurer may request
a second medical opinion or additional medical records before approving a claim. This request
should be reasonable and not used as a means of indefinitely delaying a decision beyond the
regulatory settlement timelines (see INS-001 and INS-002).

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-043
TOPIC: Specific waiting periods for named diseases or procedures
CONTENT: In addition to the general pre-existing disease waiting period, some policies apply
specific waiting periods (commonly ranging from 1 to 2 years) for particular named conditions
or procedures, such as cataract surgery, hernia repair, or joint replacement, even if these
are not pre-existing at the time of purchase. These specific waiting periods should be
distinguished from the general pre-existing disease waiting period.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-044
TOPIC: Claim intimation deadline
CONTENT: Policies generally require the policyholder or hospital to intimate (notify) the
insurer of a planned or emergency hospitalization within a specified window (commonly ranging
from immediately for planned admissions to within 24-48 hours for emergency admissions), as a
procedural requirement distinct from the final claim submission deadline. Failure to intimate
within this window does not automatically void a genuine claim but can affect processing.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-045
TOPIC: Contestability and misstatement clause
CONTENT: Many policies include a clause limiting the insurer's ability to contest a claim on
the grounds of misstatement in the proposal form after a certain number of years of
continuous coverage have passed (subject to the moratorium provisions in INS-005), balancing
the insurer's right to verify accuracy against the policyholder's interest in eventual
certainty of coverage.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-046
TOPIC: Mid-term policy cancellation
CONTENT: If a policyholder cancels a health insurance policy before its term ends, insurers
are generally required to refund the premium for the unexpired policy period on a
short-period or pro-rata basis, as specified in the policy terms, except where a claim has
already been made during that policy year, which may affect the refundable amount.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-047
TOPIC: E-insurance account and digital policy documents
CONTENT: Policyholders can hold their insurance policies, including health insurance, in a
dematerialized electronic form through an e-insurance account with an Insurance Repository,
allowing consolidated, digital access to policy documents and simplifying tasks such as
renewal, portability, and claims-related documentation.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-048
TOPIC: Health card and e-card for cashless treatment
CONTENT: Insurers or TPAs commonly issue a digital or physical health card (e-card) that a
policyholder presents at a network hospital to initiate the cashless treatment process,
enabling faster identification of the policy and coverage details compared to manual document
verification.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-049
TOPIC: Renewal premium increase and disclosure
CONTENT: Insurers are required to give advance notice of any increase in renewal premium and
must generally justify significant premium hikes to IRDAI, particularly for age-based or
portfolio-wide revisions, rather than increasing premiums arbitrarily at the time of
individual renewal without explanation.

---
CHUNK_ID: INS-050
TOPIC: Rights when a claim is only partially approved
CONTENT: When an insurer approves only part of a claimed amount, the policyholder has the
right to receive a clear, itemized explanation of which charges were disallowed and why
(such as a room-rent sub-limit deduction, a non-payable consumable, or a specific treatment
sub-limit), and can challenge the disallowed portion through the same escalation ladder
(see INS-004) used for a full denial.
